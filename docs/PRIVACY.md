# Privacy policy

*Note Organizer for Windows and for Android, made by Alexandra Moldovan. Last updated: 8 November
2026.*

Your notes stay on your device. Note Organizer has no accounts, no advertising, no analytics and
no tracking. The Windows app connects to nothing, and the Android app does not even have
permission to use the internet.

Something leaves your device only when you send it - a note you share, or a backup you save
somewhere else - or, on Android, when your phone's own backup to your Google account is on, which
you can turn off in the app. The apps collect nothing, and send nothing to me. The stores do tell
me a little about the apps, as they tell every developer: see "What the stores tell me", below.

## What the apps keep, and where

Everything you write - notes, the Trash (Settings → Recently deleted), templates, settings and
attached files - is kept in plain files on your device:

- **Windows:** a folder on your computer, `Note Organizer` in your user folder (beside
  Documents) for the installed app; uninstalling the app leaves it, and your notes, where they
  are. Settings → About shows where it is. If the app ever fails, it writes what went wrong to
  `crash_log.txt` in the same folder; the log is never sent anywhere. Each entry holds the app's
  version, your Windows version and display settings and how big your notebook is, then a
  technical trace that can name a folder, with your user name in it, or quote a few words the
  app was working on - so read it before you send it to anyone. So that no note is lost,
  the app also keeps copies of the notes file in that folder - one from just before each update,
  and a copy of any file it could not read - and never deletes them itself: a note you
  delete stays in those copies until you delete them.
- **Android:** the app's private storage, which other apps cannot read.

On Windows, spelling is checked by Windows itself, on your computer. Add to Windows dictionary
puts the word in Windows' own dictionary, which other programs use too.

The apps do not encrypt these files. They are protected by your device's own security: your
Windows account, or your phone's lock.

Your notes stay there until you delete them. Uninstalling the Android app deletes them from the
phone (a copy in your phone's backup to your Google account stays there, under Google's rules);
uninstalling the Windows app leaves its folder, which you can delete yourself. Save a backup
first if you want to keep them.

## When something leaves your device

Only when you do it, or when your phone's own backup is on:

- **Sharing a note.** A note or a share code you send goes wherever you send it - a chat, an
  email - and that app's own privacy policy applies there. A share code is compressed text, not
  encryption: anyone who has it can read the note.
- **A backup or an exported note.** Saved where you choose: a folder, a USB stick, Google Drive.
- **Opening an attached file in another app (Android).** That app is allowed to read that one
  file, and nothing else of the app's.
- **Android's own backup.** If backup is turned on in your phone's settings, Android copies the
  app's data, notes included, to your Google account - about once a day, while the phone charges on
  Wi-Fi - and puts it back when the app is installed again, or copies it straight to a new phone
  when you move to one. Google keeps that copy under its own privacy policy. The app takes part
  unless you turn this off at its Settings → Backup; from then on, Android's next backup replaces
  that copy with one small file that only says the backup is off - so the choice stays off when
  the app is restored - and holds none of your notes. As that page also says, nothing more is
  copied once the app's data passes 25 MB.
- **A page you open from the app.** Settings → About opens this policy, and, once the app is in
  a store, its page there for a review. Your browser or the store's app opens it as it would any
  other page; the app sends nothing with it.

## What the Android app may ask for

Two permissions, both for Settings → Reminders, which stays off until you turn it on:

- **Notifications**, to tell you how many notes fall on the day. It shows the count, never a
  note's title, because a notification can appear on a locked screen.
- **Running at start-up**, to set the next reminder again after the phone restarts, which clears
  every app's alarms.

It asks for nothing else - no internet, contacts, location, camera or microphone. A file you
attach, or a backup you bring in, is one you pick in Android's own file picker. Google Play may
also list a permission whose name ends in DYNAMIC_RECEIVER_NOT_EXPORTED_PERMISSION: Android's own
code adds it, and it only lets the app's parts reach each other.

## Feedback and reviews

To tell me that something is not working, or to share an idea, write a review on the app's page
in the Microsoft Store or on Google Play: Settings → About → Feedback takes you there, once the
app is published.

The store keeps your review under its own privacy policy, and shows it publicly with the name
you post under. I read reviews in the store's own pages for developers. Besides what the store
shows everyone, those pages show me a few details with each review: your country or region, the
app's version and, depending on the store, your device's make and model, its Windows or Android
version and its language. I use them only to find and fix the problem, because I have a
legitimate interest in doing so (GDPR Article 6(1)(f)). I answer in the store, publicly, as Note
Organizer. I keep nothing of a review outside the store, and you can change or delete your
review there at any time.

## What the stores tell me

The apps send me nothing. Microsoft and Google do tell every developer a little about their apps,
in their own pages for developers: Partner Center for the Microsoft Store, Play Console for Google
Play.

- **When an app fails.** If Note Organizer crashes or stops responding, Windows or Android may
  report it to Microsoft or Google, as your device's settings for diagnostic data allow. I see
  where in the app's code it failed, the app's version and, depending on the store, the kind or
  model of device, its Windows or Android version and its country or region. Microsoft calls
  this Error Reporting Data.
- **How many people use the apps.** Counts, never names: installs and active devices, for
  example, by country or region, device and Windows or Android version - and, on the Microsoft
  Store, by age group and gender, where Microsoft knows them.

None of it holds your name, your email address or your notes, and I cannot tell which report or
count is yours. I use it only to find and fix problems and to know which versions are still in
use, because I have a legitimate interest in doing so (GDPR Article 6(1)(f)). I read it in those
pages and keep none of it anywhere else. Microsoft and Google keep it under their own privacy
policies.

You can limit what your device reports: on Windows at Settings → Privacy & security →
Diagnostics & feedback, and on Android in your phone's settings for usage and diagnostics, whose
name varies from phone to phone.

## If you write to me

The app's contact address is noteorganizer.app@gmail.com, which Google Play also shows on the
app's page, and you can write to it as well. If you do, I receive your message and your email
address, and use them only to answer you and to fix what you report. Please don't send your
notes, passwords or anything sensitive: a short description of the problem is enough. If
something like that arrives, I delete it.

My mailbox is Gmail: Google keeps mail in data centres around the world, the United States among
them, and takes part in the EU–US Data Privacy Framework, which the European Commission
recognises as protecting personal data to the standard of EU law. The mailbox is protected by a
strong password and two-step sign-in, and mail reaches it over encrypted connections.

Apart from Google, which runs the mailbox, your message is never sold, and never shared with
anyone unless the law obliges me to hand it over. If it leads to a fix, I may describe the
problem in my own words in the project's public list of issues on GitHub - never with your name,
your email address or your message itself.

I delete a message from my mailbox, its Trash and my answer included, as soon as it has been
answered and its problem fixed, and at the latest 3 months after it was sent.

**Why I may keep it:** writing to me is your choice. I keep and use your message because I have
a legitimate interest in answering it and fixing what it reports, which is also why you sent it
(GDPR Article 6(1)(f)). You can object to this at any time, and I will delete your message.

## Your rights

Of everything on this page, the only thing about you that I may hold myself is a message you
wrote to me. You can ask me for a copy of it, to correct it, to delete it sooner, to stop using
it while a question about it is settled, or to send it to you in a file you can take elsewhere;
and you can object to my keeping it. Write to
[noteorganizer.app@gmail.com](mailto:noteorganizer.app@gmail.com) from the address you wrote
from, so that I can find it. I answer within a month.

A review belongs to the store you wrote it in: you change or delete it there. The failure reports
and counts the stores show me carry no name or address, so I cannot pick out yours among them;
Microsoft and Google answer for them under their own privacy policies.

No decision about you is made by a machine, and nothing about you is profiled.

If you think I have handled your data wrongly, you can complain to a data protection authority,
such as the one in the country where you live.

## Children

The apps collect nothing from anyone, children included. Please don't send a child's personal
information in an email or a review.
